Linux Boot Process: From Power Button to Login

Ever wondered what actually happens between pressing the power button and getting to your Linux login screen? Here’s a simple breakdown of what’s happening behind the scenes.

⚡ Power On
The computer receives power and starts the hardware needed to begin the boot process.

🧩 BIOS/UEFI
BIOS or UEFI is firmware built into the computer that runs before the operating system. It checks that essential hardware such as the CPU, RAM and storage is available, then looks for something it can boot from.

🔍 Hardware Detection
The system identifies the connected hardware and prepares it so the operating system can use it.

💾 Boot Device
BIOS/UEFI checks the configured boot order and finds the device containing the Linux installation, usually an SSD or hard drive.

🚀 GRUB Bootloader
GRUB is a bootloader commonly used by Linux. It loads the Linux kernel and can also give you options to select a different kernel or operating system.

🐧 Linux Kernel
The kernel is the core of Linux. It takes control of the hardware, manages resources such as CPU and memory, and starts the processes needed to bring the system up.

⚙️ systemd
Once the kernel is running, systemd becomes the first process in user space. It starts and manages the services required by the system.

🔧 Targets and Services
systemd works through targets and starts services such as networking, SSH and other processes needed to bring the system into an operational state.

🔐 Login
Once the required services are running, the login screen or terminal prompt becomes available and you can start using the system.

Once you understand what each stage is doing, the Linux boot process becomes much easier to follow.

          How to Fake a LinkedIn Post, A Short Guide.
          Firstly, start with a claim that no one can verify. "Last week, I spoke with 12 hiring managers..." no you didn't, Steven.
          Next, pick a number that SOUNDS like it might be real. 12 sounds good. Maybe.
          Stick a disclaimer in for the next bit. "I almost didn't post this..." because ov course you "almost didn't."
          Make a summary of what you "learned," as if this wasn't a completely made up scenario in your head. Astounding work.
          Don't forget to throw in a fake quote that no real human being would ever say, like, "We’re really just looking for someone who can take ownership."
          Don't ever mention specifics because then people can hold you accountable. Be as vague as you possible can to get away with it.
          End with a soft CTA so people will message you!
          If this is what passes for #content for you, then you should probably get off LinkedIn and touch grass. It's 2026. Let's leave ChatGPT writing our LinkedIn updates wholesale and coming up with spurious numbers no one can verify back in 2025. You're better than that. Stop lying to people.
